The current UAE threat map

To explain this proactive security posture, it will be useful to map out the threats surrounding the UAE and how its leadership interprets these threats. The UAE, Khan contends, is in the midst of a region of the least politically stable world and in whose neighbourhood, wars are more likely to break than not. [1] An analysis of the UAE threat map reveals that Political Islam was the key culprit in this militarization drive in many of its forms. The UAE may have learned to co-exist with the threat posed by the Islamic Revolution of Iran, which was intent upon spreading a militant form of Islam beyond its borders but there were other equally pressing sources of concern; the strand in Political Islam which the UAE is most opposed to is one coming from the Muslim Brotherhood movement. Bell notes that Political Islam is rejected as being "utopian" or "moralist". [2] This rejection is not only occasioned by the UAE's secular orientation to create a division between the affairs of the State and the State's official religion but also by the perception of the Islah movement in the UAE and elsewhere as eventually casting doubt on the very legitimacy of the Emirati political system. [3] Islah in the UAE is seen as an extension of "the Islamist core" Muslim Brotherhood movement in Yemen, Qatar, Turkey, and Egypt, [4] which is seen as an intermestic threat, that is, a domestic issue with international dimensions and a "malignant disease" with direct implications for the homefront.
