Where does Use of Military Power Literature come from?

As stated earlier, military power use is a complex phenomenon. Paret explains that no state's strategic plans rest on a constellation of military factors alone. War is a decision taken at a particular point in time that affects a specific land area and a designated enemy, which requires full military readiness. The decision to engage in war is driven by political motives and has consequences for those on the ground and the politicians who took the decision. [1] Paret says that "every segment of military policy and action, down to the level of small-unit tactics, is influenced by any number of factors beyond the strictly military." To understand why men fought in a certain fashion we must know something of their circumstances before they heaved the first rock or fired the first shot." [2] Discussing complexity, decision making, motivation, and leadership puts the use of military power in the realm of decision making and explains the conditions that lead to war and its ramifications. It calls for a rich analysis where a complex of factors come into play, involving military and non-military actors. In this sense, too, this theoretical strand is multilayered, multilevel, and contextual, adds a certain richness to the conversation on state survival, and unpacks the apparent state of anarchy from the narrow standpoint of this mid-level theory. Though the state ostensibly takes the decision to go to war, it is leaders who are at the origin of this decision. Therefore, a study of their motives harks back to their beliefs and levels of certainty about winning a war. [3]

[1] Paret, P. (2020). Understanding war: Essays on Clausewitz and the History of Military Power. Princeton University Press.
